William KM Lai, PhD

Assistant Professor of Biochemistry · Jacobs School of Medicine and Biomedical Sciences · University at Buffalo (SUNY)

The Lai Lab investigates both the molecular grammar through which transcription factors, chromatin remodelers, and transcriptional machinery organize the eukaryotic genome and how perturbations to this regulatory architecture drive cellular stress responses and human disease. Our group pairs high-resolution wet-bench biochemical assays with scalable machine learning, generative structural modeling, and open-source software engineering.

At the experimental bench, we apply single-base-pair footprinting and nucleosome-phasing technologies including ChIP-exo, WhIP-exo, Benzonase-seq, and PB-exo alongside multi-modal in situ single-cell profiling on the AVITI24 platform across yeast, mammalian, and clinical specimen models. To decode the massive dimensionality of these atlases, we build sequence motif consolidation algorithms (GenoCLAMP), causal gene regulatory network frameworks grounded in orthogonal molecular evidence (SETIA), and adversarial explainable AI methods (APSO / ChromEnhancer) that reveal chromatin rules without sequence bias.

Complementing our epigenomics program, we develop continuous-time consistency distillation methods (Blitz and MS-sCD) that accelerate diffusion-based biomolecular structure prediction models such as AlphaFold3 and Boltz-2 from hundreds of denoising steps down to 8–16 steps, paired with containerized high-throughput computing workflows (OmniFold). Across every project, we are committed to FAIR science gateways, developing PEGR, ScriptManager, GenoPipe, and STENCIL so experimentalists and computational biologists alike can execute rigorous, reproducible analyses at scale. Our research and cyberinfrastructure are supported by an NIH NIGMS R35 (MIRA) and national NSF ACCESS supercomputing allocations.

Research Pillars

Full research overview →
Thrust 01

High-Resolution Epigenomic Assays & Reference Atlases

Mapping TF footprints, nucleosome phasing, PIC assembly, and single-cell stress states via ChIP-exo, WhIP-exo, Benzonase-seq, PB-exo, and AVITI24, including the >400-protein Yeast Epigenome and 887-TF Human PCRP atlases.

Thrust 02

Bioinformatic Algorithm Development

Statistical, alignment, motif-consolidation, causal GRN, and interpretable ML algorithms for high-resolution regulatory genomics (ArchAlign, ChExMix, GenoPipe, GenoCLAMP, SETIA, APSO/ChromEnhancer).

Explore thrust →
Thrust 03

Generative Diffusion Distillation & Biomolecular Modeling

Accelerating AlphaFold3 and Boltz-2 diffusion down to 8–16 steps via MS-sCD and Blitz alongside decoupled CPU/GPU structure prediction and design containers (OmniFold).

Explore thrust →
Thrust 04

Open-Science Gateways, Accessibility & HPC Education

Lowering computational barriers through FAIR graphical workflows, browser portals, Open OnDemand apps, and federated cloud classrooms (ScriptManager, PEGR, STENCIL, ProteinStructure-OOD, EGC-jupyterhub).
