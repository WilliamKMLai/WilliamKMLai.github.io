Teaching & Educational Gateways

In the Department of Biochemistry at the Jacobs School of Medicine and Biomedical Sciences, University at Buffalo (SUNY), our educational mission integrates quantitative genomics, machine learning interpretability, and reproducible high-performance computing into biomedical sciences and medical training. We emphasize lowering technical barriers so experimental life scientists can interrogate high-dimensional omics and structural datasets with rigor.

Zero-Cost Scalable Bioinformatics Classrooms (EGC-jupyterhub)

To support hands-on computational biology and AI instruction without placing hardware or administrative burdens on host institutions, we architected an open-source cloud classroom deployment (EpiGenomicsCode/EGC-jupyterhub) deployed across both 200+-student university courses and K–12 computational thinking living labs at zero cost to host schools.

JupyterHub (via The Littlest JupyterHub) serves as a uniform browser-based scientific gateway reflecting current best-practices in reproducible bioinformatics. Cloud compute instances are provisioned through the NSF-funded ACCESS initiative on Jetstream2, while the NCSA-supported CILogon initiative provides federated institutional credentialing—allowing students to authenticate directly with their university credentials and eliminating manual account management for instructors.

  • SC26 Workshop Case Study (2026): “Empowering K-12 Education through HPC-enabled AI and Computational Thinking: A Living Lab Case Study” (Thirteenth SC Workshop on Best Practices for HPC Training and Education [BPHTE26], 2026)
  • Classroom Deployment Recipes: https://github.com/EpiGenomicsCode/EGC-jupyterhub
  • Science Gateway Platform: The Littlest JupyterHub (TLJH)
  • Cloud HPC Infrastructure: NSF ACCESS & Jetstream2
  • Federated Institutional Authentication: NCSA CILogon

Prior Courses Taught

2023 – 2025 Cornell University · Course Instructor

CB 2010 / CB 6010: Introduction to Computational Biology

Team-taught undergraduate and graduate survey course introducing students across the life sciences to how computational and quantitative methods transform biological discovery. Delivered through two weekly lectures and one weekly hands-on computational practical session, the curriculum trained students to manipulate genome sequences, functional genomics datasets, biological images, and protein structures.

Practical modules built foundational programming fluency and quantitative problem-solving across genome analysis, neurobiology, ecology, physiology, and evolutionary modeling, culminating in an independent computational term project executed on our Jetstream2 JupyterHub gateway.

2022 – 2026 Cornell University · Course Instructor

BIOG 2290: Introduction to Research in Computational Biology

Faculty-mentored independent computational biology research course training undergraduates in hypothesis formulation, reproducible command-line and Python workflows, version-controlled software engineering, and large-scale epigenomic data quality control.

Alongside classroom instruction, Dr. Lai served as Co-Chair of the Computational Biology Undergraduate Honors Thesis Program (2022–2026) and freshman faculty advisor at Cornell University, as well as a member of the NSF ACCESS External Advisory Board (2023–Present).

Molecular and Cellular Biology II

Co-taught with B. Franklin Pugh, PhD in the Department of Biochemistry and Molecular Biology at Pennsylvania State University.
